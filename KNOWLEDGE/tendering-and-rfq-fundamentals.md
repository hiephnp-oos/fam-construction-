# Tendering and RFQ fundamentals

Reusable workflow principles synthesized from recurring FAM tender discussions. Package-specific instructions always take precedence.

## 1. Establish the bid basis
- Identify the latest issued tender documents, addenda, drawing/specification revisions, BOQ and submission instructions.
- Record document title, revision/date and source. Do not assume files with similar names are equivalent.
- Confirm scope boundaries, work locations, interfaces, exclusions, programme, access, working-hour constraints, temporary works, testing, handover and maintenance obligations where applicable.
- Separate explicit requirements from bidder assumptions and proposed alternatives.

## 2. Reconcile scope before pricing
- Cross-check BOQ items against drawings, specifications, schedules and written clarifications.
- Flag missing items, duplicated descriptions, inconsistent units, quantity differences and conflicts between documents.
- Do not silently choose which document governs. Raise a written clarification where the discrepancy can affect price, compliance, quality or delivery.
- Maintain a traceable clarification log: reference, document/revision, location/item, issue, impact, question, response and resulting action.
- Distinguish quantity discrepancy from scope omission and from a measurement-method difference.

## 3. Clarification communication
- State the exact document, revision, drawing/detail, BOQ item or clause.
- Describe the discrepancy neutrally and explain the bid/compliance impact without asserting an unsupported interpretation.
- Ask for a specific resolution, such as confirmation of governing revision, revised BOQ, missing detail, measurement basis or responsibility boundary.
- Request that material answers be issued to all bidders when fairness or common pricing basis is affected; follow the actual procurement instructions.
- Keep the response and its effect on price, scope and programme traceable.

## 4. Build a controlled submission
- Check compliance against the invitation and submission checklist.
- Separate technical submission, commercial submission, qualifications, assumptions, exclusions, alternatives and supporting evidence as requested.
- Ensure each price maps to a defined scope, unit, quantity basis and inclusions.
- Do not imply that a proposed material, brand, method or deviation is approved unless written acceptance exists.
- Check arithmetic, units, totals, validity, taxes, currency, schedule and required signatures before submission.

## 5. Bid risk and decision records
- Identify unresolved items that could materially change cost, time, compliance or liability.
- State the pricing treatment for each unresolved item: included, excluded, provisional, qualified or pending clarification, only where tender rules permit.
- Keep internal risk notes distinct from statements submitted to the client.
- After clarification/addendum, re-check affected BOQ lines, technical compliance, price build-up and submission documents.

## 6. Evidence and limits
This file provides process guidance, not a legal rule about tender fairness or document precedence. Follow the actual tender conditions and applicable law. Verify legal/procurement interpretations from current authoritative sources before relying on them.
