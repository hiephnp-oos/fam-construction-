# Construction cost and unit-rate principles

Reusable estimating principles synthesized from FAM cost discussions. This document does not contain current market prices or approved rate libraries.

## 1. Define the rate before calculating
For each item, establish:
- Work description, location, specification, quality/finish and access conditions.
- Unit of measurement and measurement rules.
- Included operations, interfaces, tolerances, testing and handover.
- Whether the requested figure is material supply, installation labour, plant/equipment, subcontract package or complete installed rate.
- Date, geography, currency, tax treatment and quotation basis.

A rate is not comparable until scope, unit and inclusions are comparable.

## 2. Build up transparently
A typical analytical structure is:
- Materials, including documented wastage where applicable.
- Labour by trade and productivity assumption.
- Plant, tools and equipment.
- Transport, loading/unloading, handling and disposal where applicable.
- Subcontract/specialist costs.
- Site overheads, company overheads, risk/contingency and profit, shown separately according to the estimating convention.
- Taxes and statutory charges treated according to the requested basis.

Avoid double counting: for example, verify whether supplier prices already include delivery, installation, accessories or tax.

## 3. State calculation basis
- Show quantity conversion and unit conversion explicitly.
- Separate measured quantity from allowance/wastage.
- Record productivity and crew assumptions when they materially affect the result.
- Identify exclusions and provisional assumptions.
- For composite items, list included layers/components and installation steps.
- Check dimensional consistency before presenting a unit rate.

## 4. Market-rate research
When a current market rate is requested:
- Search for comparable local quotations or published price references, prioritizing dated supplier/contractor sources and official price publications where relevant.
- Use web research tools only when current external information is needed; use extraction tools for relevant long pages or documents that search snippets cannot establish.
- Record source, date, location, specification, unit, tax and inclusions. Distinguish asking price, material-only price, labour quote and completed-work rate.
- Use multiple comparable references where practical; explain why a reference is not directly comparable.
- Do not manufacture a precise rate from a single weak reference. Give a range or request missing scope details when uncertainty is material.
- Do not use an old project quotation as a current market benchmark without qualification.

## 5. Presenting an estimate
For each result, state the rate basis, key assumptions, inclusions/exclusions, evidence quality and date. Keep calculated estimates distinct from sourced market observations. If the user asks for a quick budget figure, label it as preliminary and identify the main uncertainty drivers.

## Limits
This is a method guide, not a cost database. Rates vary by date, location, specification, access, quantity, contractor capacity and market conditions. Verify current prices before tender submission.
