# Landscape, softscape and irrigation fundamentals

Reusable reasoning framework synthesized from FAM landscape discussions. It is not a design specification or a substitute for project drawings, horticultural expertise, hydraulic calculations or supplier data.

## 1. Define the landscape scope
- Separate softscape, hardscape interfaces, irrigation, soil preparation, planting, establishment, maintenance and replacement obligations.
- Identify who supplies, installs, tests, commissions and maintains each component.
- Check interfaces with levels, drainage, waterproofing, utilities, lighting, paving, structures and access.
- Distinguish installation scope from maintenance-period obligations and acceptance criteria.

## 2. Planting and soil
- Read plant schedule, plans, details and specifications together; verify botanical/common name, size, form, quantity, spacing, root condition and location.
- Suitability depends on site exposure, climate, salinity/wind where relevant, soil condition, drainage, water availability, maintenance capacity and intended visual function.
- Do not infer soil mix ratios, fertilizer rates, plant substitutions or establishment periods from generic practice. Require project specification or verified agronomic/supplier basis.
- Clarify whether soil improvement, excavation, disposal, backfill, staking, mulch, watering-in and plant replacement are included.

## 3. Irrigation system reasoning
- Start from planting demand and site constraints, then trace source → treatment/storage (if specified) → pumping/pressure control (if needed) → distribution → control/valves → emitters/sprinklers → drainage/flush points.
- Identify irrigation method by intended application and plant/area type; do not assume drip, spray or rotor systems are interchangeable.
- Check zoning, flow, operating pressure, elevation changes, pipe route, filtration, water quality, control strategy, isolation, maintenance access and power/control interfaces.
- Verify hydraulic performance using design calculations and manufacturer data. Never invent pipe sizes, pump duty, pressure, flow or spacing.
- Review coverage and overspray against paving, façades, pedestrian areas, water-sensitive elements and adjacent zones.
- Clarify testing, flushing, commissioning, as-built records, operator training and maintenance responsibilities.

## 4. Drawing and material review
- Cross-check irrigation layout, planting plan, schedules, details, legends and specifications; note revisions.
- Map each material/equipment item to a location and function. Record brand/model only when specified or supported by approved submittal.
- Treat a suggested brand or equivalent as a proposal until compliance and approval are confirmed.
- Record conflicts, missing details and unclear interfaces as RFIs/clarifications rather than silently filling gaps.

## 5. Maintenance and handover
- Identify maintenance duration, visit frequency, watering, pruning, weeding, fertilization, pest/disease response, replacement thresholds and reporting requirements.
- Confirm responsibility for water, electricity, consumables, access and damage caused by other trades.
- Tie acceptance to measurable project criteria where provided; do not invent survival rates or performance thresholds.

## Evidence boundary
The above is a review checklist distilled from conversation patterns. Site-specific horticultural and hydraulic decisions require current project data and qualified verification.
